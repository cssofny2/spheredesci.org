Interferometry uses the superposition of waves to compare their relative phase. When waves from two paths are recombined, changes in phase can alter the observed interference pattern. The relationship between that pattern and a physical quantity depends on the optical arrangement and calibration.

## Sensitivity comes with controls

Interferometric systems can detect small changes in optical path, but environmental vibration, air properties, alignment, surface reflectivity, and signal processing can influence an observation. A sensitivity specification is not a complete uncertainty budget.

## Interferometry and vibration

Some non-contact vibration instruments infer surface motion from changes in reflected light. Their usable result depends on beam geometry, instrument calibration, the measured point, and the analysis method. The project's proposed apparatus would need to specify these details before any sensitivity claim could be assessed.

Interferometry is distinct from the resonance itself: it can be one way to observe motion, while the mechanical system's mode and frequency are properties of the object and its conditions.
