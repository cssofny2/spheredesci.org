A vacuum chamber is a sealed enclosure in which gas pressure can be reduced below ambient pressure. The required pressure depends on the experiment: removing gas may reduce some forms of damping or convection, but it does not automatically create a perfectly stable environment.

## Design details matter

Chamber material, seals, pumps, vibration transmission, electrical feedthroughs, thermal control, and cleanliness can affect an experiment. Pump operation can itself introduce vibration or acoustic noise. A chamber's pressure rating does not describe the full measurement environment.

## Relevance to resonance measurements

If a proposed measurement uses a chamber, the protocol should state the environmental quantity it is intended to control and how that quantity will be measured. Temperature, support conditions, electromagnetic exposure, and sensor alignment still need separate treatment.
