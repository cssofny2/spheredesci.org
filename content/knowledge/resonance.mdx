Resonance describes a system's response when a periodic input drives it near a frequency at which energy couples efficiently into one of its modes. The resulting response depends on the system, its damping, how it is driven, and how it is observed.

## Natural modes and frequency

An elastic object can support several vibration patterns, each with a characteristic natural frequency. Shape, material properties, boundary conditions, and support affect those modes. A hollow shell and a solid sphere therefore do not share the same mode structure just because both are spherical.

## Damping and measurement

Damping dissipates energy and affects both the amplitude and width of a resonance. Instrument response, excitation level, sensor position, and data-analysis method can also affect the frequency estimate. A measured peak should be tied to a defined mode and procedure.

## What a frequency shift does—and does not—show

A shift between measurements can arise from a physical change, but also from environmental conditions, mounting, calibration, or analysis. A controlled comparison needs a pre-specified model and uncertainty budget.
