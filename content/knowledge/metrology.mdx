Metrology is the science of measurement. It covers the practical and theoretical work needed to define a quantity, realize its unit, compare instruments with references, and communicate how uncertain a measured value is.

## Measurement is more than reading a display

A measurement result depends on a stated measurand—the quantity intended to be measured—as well as the method, instrument, environment, and model used to interpret observations. If those are unclear, two reported values may not describe the same thing.

## Calibration and traceability

Calibration establishes a relationship between an instrument's indications and reference values, including associated uncertainty. Traceability is a documented chain of calibrations connecting a result to an appropriate reference. Neither guarantees that an instrument is suitable for every use or that all sources of bias have been controlled.

## Uncertainty belongs with the result

Measurement uncertainty describes the dispersion of values that could reasonably be attributed to a measurand, given the information and model used. It is not simply a synonym for a mistake. A useful <a href="/research/frequency-measurement-error-budget/">uncertainty budget</a> makes important contributions and assumptions inspectable.

## Relevance to resonance research

Resonance measurements can be sensitive to temperature, supports, excitation, alignment, timing, and data processing. A proposed comparison needs a definition of the mode and measurement window, calibration evidence, controls, and a plan for uncertainty before readers can judge whether a difference would be meaningful.

The Project S.P.H.E.R.E. work described on this site is a proposal, not a completed measurement campaign.
