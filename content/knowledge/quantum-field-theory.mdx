Quantum field theory (QFT) is a framework for describing quantum fields and their interactions, commonly used together with special relativity. In this framework, fields are central physical quantities; particles are represented by quantized excitations of fields in the theories where that description applies.

## A framework, not one universal equation

QFT includes different mathematical models with different fields, symmetries, parameters, and domains of application. A claim about QFT therefore needs to identify a specific theory, calculation, and testable prediction rather than invoke the framework as a general explanation.

## Relation to measurements

Modern measurements can test consequences of specific physical theories. Atomic clocks, particle experiments, and interferometers may be compared with precise predictions, but an instrument's sensitivity does not itself imply a new effect.

## Scope of Project S.P.H.E.R.E.

Project S.P.H.E.R.E. proposes controlled measurements of resonant frequencies and possible confounders. The current proposal does not establish a conflict with established physics or claim to test a particular QFT prediction. Any future connection would require a precise model and independent review.
