Hardware for synchronized resonance measurements

The design calls for concurrent readout, verified timing and a calibration trail. These examples illustrate hardware categories; they are not a claim that SPHERE owns or uses these exact devices.

Added October 9, 2026 · Proposal stage · Draft for public review

Status: proposal for public review. This page contains no experiment results, community responses, installed-hardware claims or published findings. Hypotheses are not findings, and nothing here amends the current draft LOC-1 protocol.

Examples are named only to make the categories concrete. SPHERE has not selected, ordered or installed any of them, and no vendor has endorsed or participated in the project.

About product images

This page deliberately shows no vendor photographs because reuse rights for third-party product images have not been cleared. Project photographs of the actual apparatus, with credits and exact model identifiers, will be added when they exist. For the staged budget, alternatives and used-market ranges, see the equipment procurement plan. That plan lists a scanning 3D vibrometer, which visits measurement points in sequence and is not by itself a four-object simultaneous readout.

Optical readout

A laser Doppler vibrometer provides non-contact vibration readout. Polytec describes resonance-frequency and frequency-response measurements using this technique. For four simultaneous objects, confirm four suitable concurrent readout paths, frequency bandwidth, demodulator output, optical geometry and signal quality. Rental or partner-lab access remains an option rather than assuming a purchase.

Source: Polytec: Laser Doppler vibrometry

Acquisition hardware

The DAQ must support the required per-channel bandwidth, true simultaneous sampling where required, compatible analog inputs, reference-clock routing and hardware triggering. A photograph of a PXIe module is not verification of these capabilities. Exact model selection remains open pending measured requirements.

Source: NI: Sample Clock Synchronization

Frequency reference

The SRS PRS10 is a 10 MHz rubidium-disciplined crystal oscillator described for synchronization and precision frequency measurement. Its inclusion here is illustrative, not a recommendation that it automatically meets the complete timing budget. Check electrical compatibility, distribution, warm-up, lock behavior and calibration documentation.

Source: SRS: PRS10 rubidium frequency standard

Supporting measurement chain

  • Matched and characterized copper spheres with reproducible fixtures.
  • Calibrated temperature sensors at multiple locations, enclosure control and gradient logging.
  • Excitation hardware whose mechanical and electrical crosstalk can be characterized.
  • Shielding and isolation assessed against measured noise, not assumed perfect.
  • Timing distribution, test-signal source and documented cable/analog-path delays.

Before procurement

Define the observable first. A vibrometer’s mechanical velocity signal is not a network analyzer’s electromagnetic transfer measurement. If both are included, give each a separate acquisition and analysis specification. Ask vendors to demonstrate all channels simultaneously at the proposed bandwidth and export raw records for independent timing checks.

Public procurement question

Have you rented instrumentation, shared a university lab or built a synchronized DAQ? Explain what worked, hidden costs, integration problems and what you would do differently.
