Blinding in physics experiments: blind analysis explained

Blinding hides information that could bias a measurement or analysis, so that the result cannot be nudged, even unintentionally, toward what the researcher expects.

Added October 14, 2026 · Educational explainer

Status: educational explainer. This page reports no experiment results. Project S.P.H.E.R.E. is at the proposal stage, hypotheses are not findings, and nothing here amends the draft LOC-1 protocol.

Why blinding exists

Researchers are human. Knowing which condition is which can influence decisions about cuts, corrections and when to stop. Blinding removes that knowledge until the analysis is fixed.

Forms of blinding

  • Blinded conditions: sample labels are coded so the operator does not know which is which.
  • Blind analysis: the key quantity is offset or hidden while the analysis method is finalized, then unblinded once.
  • Independent analysts: one team collects, another analyzes.

Limits

Blinding cannot fix a poor design or uncontrolled systematic effects, and in some setups it is hard to hide a condition. It should be paired with preregistration and a clear uncertainty budget.

In the draft protocol

The LOC-1 draft proposes blinded location comparisons. It is a draft for public review; no blinded data collection has occurred.
