What is resonant frequency?

Every elastic object has natural frequencies at which it vibrates easily. Drive it at one of them and the response grows large: that is resonance.

Added October 14, 2026 · Educational explainer

Status: educational explainer. This page reports no experiment results. Project S.P.H.E.R.E. is at the proposal stage, hypotheses are not findings, and nothing here amends the draft LOC-1 protocol.

Natural frequency and resonance

Strike a bell, a glass or a tuning fork and it rings at particular pitches. Those are its natural (resonant) frequencies. They depend on the object's shape, size, mass distribution and the stiffness of its material. A periodic push that matches a natural frequency transfers energy efficiently, so the vibration builds up until losses balance the input.

Key quantities

  • Frequency (f): cycles per second, in hertz (Hz).
  • Quality factor (Q): how sharply peaked the resonance is; high Q means low damping.
  • Mode: a specific vibration pattern with its own frequency.

Why frequency is a good thing to measure

Frequency can be measured very precisely against a stable clock, so small shifts in a resonance can be detected. The same property makes it sensitive to temperature, mounting and other disturbances, which must be controlled or recorded.

The proposed experiment compares the measured resonant frequencies of controlled copper spheres at different locations. Whether any location dependence exists is the hypothesis being tested, not an established result. Next: vibration modes of a sphere. Background: Wikipedia: Resonance.
