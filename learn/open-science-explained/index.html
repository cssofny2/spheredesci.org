Open science explained

Open science means making the methods, data and conclusions of research available so that others can inspect, challenge and build on them.

Added October 14, 2026 · Educational explainer

Status: educational explainer. This page reports no experiment results. Project S.P.H.E.R.E. is at the proposal stage, hypotheses are not findings, and nothing here amends the draft LOC-1 protocol.

The core idea

Research is easier to trust when it can be checked. Open science is a collection of habits that make checking possible: sharing the plan, the raw measurements, the analysis code and the final write-up, ideally in formats other people can actually use.

Five practices to know

  • Open access: papers readable without a subscription.
  • Open data: raw or processed measurements with documentation.
  • Open methods and code: enough detail to repeat the work.
  • Preregistration: a time-stamped plan made before the data exist. See preregistration explained.
  • Open review: critique that is visible and citable.

Limits and trade-offs

Openness takes effort and has costs: data need cleaning and documenting, some data involve privacy, and sharing does not guarantee that anyone will check it. Good open science states what is and is not shared and why.

Open science at S.P.H.E.R.E.

The project publishes its draft protocol, budget plan and research pages for public comment and invites outside review through the collaboration page. Everything remains a proposal until a real, dated record says otherwise. More background is available at Wikipedia: Open science.
