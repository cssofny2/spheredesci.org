What is DeSci? Decentralized science, explained

DeSci is shorthand for decentralized science: a set of ideas for making research funding, review and data sharing more open and less dependent on a few gatekeepers.

Added October 14, 2026 · Educational explainer

Status: educational explainer. This page reports no experiment results. Project S.P.H.E.R.E. is at the proposal stage, hypotheses are not findings, and nothing here amends the draft LOC-1 protocol.

What does DeSci mean?

Decentralized science (DeSci) is an umbrella term for efforts to run research in the open: publicly visible plans, openly shared data and code, transparent funding, and review that is not limited to a single journal or institution. Some DeSci projects use blockchain tools; many of the underlying ideas, such as open access and preregistration, predate them and need no special technology.

Common DeSci practices

  • Open protocols: publish the plan before collecting data.
  • Open data and code: let others re-run the analysis.
  • Transparent funding: show where money comes from and what it buys.
  • Independent review: invite critique from outside the project.
  • Publish what you find: including null and inconclusive results.

Why people are interested

Supporters point to slow publishing cycles, paywalled papers, funding concentrated in few hands, and studies that are hard to replicate. DeSci proposals aim to address these by making the research record public and auditable. Critics note that openness does not by itself make research correct, and that governance and incentives still need careful design.

How S.P.H.E.R.E. uses the idea

Project S.P.H.E.R.E. is an independent, open research proposal. It applies the practical parts of DeSci: a public draft protocol, a published budget plan, and a commitment to share results either way. It does not issue a token, coin or on-chain asset, and contributions do not buy equity, royalties or a financial return. The project is at the proposal stage and has no measurement results yet.

Read the DeSci whitepaper, the research charter and the transparency page for specifics.
