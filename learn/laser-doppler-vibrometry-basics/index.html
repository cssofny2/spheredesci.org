Laser Doppler vibrometer basics

A laser Doppler vibrometer measures how fast a surface moves by analyzing the shift in frequency of laser light reflected from it.

Added October 14, 2026 · Educational explainer

Status: educational explainer. This page reports no experiment results. Project S.P.H.E.R.E. is at the proposal stage, hypotheses are not findings, and nothing here amends the draft LOC-1 protocol.

How it works

When light reflects off a moving surface its frequency shifts slightly (the Doppler effect). The instrument compares the reflected beam with a reference beam, and the interference reveals the surface velocity or displacement over time. No sensor touches the object.

Why non-contact matters

Attaching an accelerometer adds mass and can change the very frequency being measured, especially on a small sphere. Optical measurement avoids that loading, though it introduces its own needs: stable alignment, adequate surface reflectivity and vibration isolation of the instrument itself.

Common error sources

  • Beam angle and measurement-point position.
  • Poor reflectivity or speckle noise.
  • Instrument or table vibration.
  • Timing and clock errors when comparing several instruments.

In this project

The synchronized-measurement pages discuss illustrative vibrometer setups. No instrument has been purchased or installed on the basis of these pages. See the hardware guide and the equipment plan. Background: Wikipedia: Laser Doppler vibrometer.
