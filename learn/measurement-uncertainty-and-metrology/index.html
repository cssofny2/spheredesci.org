Measurement uncertainty and metrology: an uncertainty budget primer

Every measurement has an uncertainty. Metrology is the science of measurement; an uncertainty budget is the list of everything that limits how well you know the answer.

Added October 14, 2026 · Educational explainer

Status: educational explainer. This page reports no experiment results. Project S.P.H.E.R.E. is at the proposal stage, hypotheses are not findings, and nothing here amends the draft LOC-1 protocol.

Random vs. systematic error

Random errors scatter results around the true value and shrink as you average more data. Systematic errors push results the same way every time and do not average away; they must be found, bounded or corrected.

What an uncertainty budget is

An uncertainty budget lists each contribution (temperature, mounting, timing, alignment, electronics and so on), estimates its size, and combines them into a total. The international guide for this is the Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement (GUM), published through the BIPM JCGM.

Why it prevents false discoveries

A small difference between two measurements is only meaningful if it is larger than the combined uncertainty. Without a budget it is easy to mistake an instrument artifact for a real effect.

Applied to S.P.H.E.R.E.

The project's illustrative frequency measurement error budget shows how timing, drift and temperature would be tracked. It is a planning document, not a measured budget.
