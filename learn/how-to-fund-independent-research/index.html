How independent research gets funded

Researchers outside large institutions piece together support from several sources. What keeps any of them trustworthy is transparency about money and independence.

Added October 14, 2026 · Educational explainer

Status: educational explainer. This page reports no experiment results. Project S.P.H.E.R.E. is at the proposal stage, hypotheses are not findings, and nothing here amends the draft LOC-1 protocol.

Common sources

  • Grants from government agencies and private foundations.
  • Institutional support or in-kind equipment and lab access.
  • Individual donations and community fundraising.
  • Fiscal sponsorship by an existing nonprofit.

Transparency practices

Publish a budget before spending, state what each line buys, disclose funders and any conditions, and report how money was actually used. Separating funding from the right to influence conclusions protects the research.

Cautions for donors

Be wary of projects that promise returns, tokens or guaranteed results in exchange for support. Genuine research funding does not guarantee a finding.

S.P.H.E.R.E.'s position

The project is at the proposal stage. The transparency page shows a draft budget plan; it is not a record of funds received. Contributions do not purchase equity, tokens, royalties or a financial return. To help in other ways, see collaborate.
