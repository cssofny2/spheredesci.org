The replication crisis and reproducibility, explained

In several fields, attempts to repeat published studies have often failed to match the original results. Here is why that happens and what helps.

Added October 14, 2026 · Educational explainer

Status: educational explainer. This page reports no experiment results. Project S.P.H.E.R.E. is at the proposal stage, hypotheses are not findings, and nothing here amends the draft LOC-1 protocol.

Replication vs. reproducibility

Terms vary, but a common usage is: reproducing means re-running the same analysis on the same data and getting the same numbers; replicating means collecting new data with the same method and checking whether the finding holds.

Why findings fail to replicate

  • Small samples and noisy measurements.
  • Flexible analysis choices made after seeing data.
  • Publication bias: positive results get published more often than null ones.
  • Uncontrolled differences in equipment, environment or procedure.
  • Honest error, and occasionally misconduct.

What helps

Preregistered plans, blinded analysis, shared data and code, uncertainty budgets, independent replication and publishing null results all make unreliable findings easier to catch. See preregistration, null results and measurement uncertainty.

Why this matters for S.P.H.E.R.E.

The project proposes a surprising-sounding question (whether location alone changes a controlled object's measured resonance). Such a question demands strong controls and independent repetition, which is why the draft protocol emphasizes blinding, reference artifacts and an error budget. See the error budget page. Background: Wikipedia: Replication crisis.
