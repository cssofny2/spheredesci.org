Testing the Locational Variable Hypothesis

Project S.P.H.E.R.E. is an open, independent research project asking a simple question: if you move an object and change nothing else, does its resonant frequency change? Standard physics predicts it will not. We are designing a preregistered, blinded test to find out, and we will publish the result either way.

The Hypothesis and the Null

Standard physics holds that an object's resonance is set by its material, shape, temperature, and surroundings, not by where it is. The Bashar material proposes that location is part of an object's own vibrational makeup. S.P.H.E.R.E. tests that proposal as a hypothesis, not as an assumption.

The Hypothesis

In the Bashar framework, an object's location is described as an intrinsic vibrational variable rather than an external coordinate. If that were true, moving an object might shift its resonant frequency by a small, repeatable amount. This idea is unverified.

The Prediction Being Tested

The source material points to effects above 230 kHz. We will select a well-identified resonance mode in that band and look for a frequency difference (Δf) between two positions larger than a preregistered smallest effect of interest.

The Null Expectation

Standard physics predicts Δf = 0 once temperature, mounting, and other ordinary effects are controlled. If that is what we find, we will publish it as a complete result. Teleportation or "shifting" claims are outside this project's scope.

The First Experiment (LOC-1)

Small frequency shifts are easy to produce by accident through temperature changes, mounting stress, RF interference, or clock drift. The protocol is designed to rule those out before anything is attributed to location.

  • 1

    Matched Reference Sphere

    Two matched hollow copper spheres share one shielded, temperature-controlled enclosure. One never moves. The test sphere is measured as a ratio against it, which cancels shared drift.

  • 2

    Blinded, Randomized Placement

    The test sphere is moved between two marked positions 0.40 m apart on a randomized schedule held by an independent person. The analyst sees only coded labels. Sham moves, rotations, and a dummy object serve as null tests.

  • 3

    Preregistered Analysis

    The analysis rules for Δf = fB − fA, including the smallest effect of interest, α = 0.01, and an equivalence test for a null result, are frozen on OSF before data collection. Any positive signal must be independently replicated before it is claimed.

Metrology Approach

Laser Doppler Vibrometry

Rental / partner lab

Non-contact optical readout with no mass loading on the sphere. Instrument time will be rented or arranged through a partner lab rather than purchased.

Thermal Control Enclosure

Project-built

Millikelvin-level temperature control and logging, because copper's resonance shifts measurably with temperature. The temperature coefficient is measured during calibration.

Shielded Enclosure

Project-built

Reduces ambient radio noise in the measurement band. Shielding performance will be measured and published.

GPS / Rubidium Timebase

Shared reference

Every instrument locks to one frequency reference, so clock drift cannot masquerade as a location effect.

Funding and Transparency

S.P.H.E.R.E. is funded in stages, starting with conventional, non-investment support: donations, sponsorships, grants, and reward-based crowdfunding. Decentralized-science tools may be used later for transparency and community input, but only after the legal entity, financial controls, and first results are in place.

No Tokens, No Equity

Contributions do not buy equity, tokens, royalties, or any financial return. There is no token sale, and none is planned for the founding phase.

Transparent Treasury

Funds will be held in a dedicated project account with documented controls. Spending summaries are published quarterly, and donor funds are kept separate from founder capital.

Open-Source Data

Raw measurements, environmental logs, and analysis code will be published with a permanent DOI, so anyone can check or repeat the analysis.
