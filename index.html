Does every object carry a built-in record of where it is?

Project S.P.H.E.R.E. is a plan to test one idea in a real lab. Some researchers think an object holds information about its own place in space and time. Standard physics says it does not. We want to find out with very sensitive instruments, and we will publish the answer either way.

The project in plain words

No physics background needed. Here is what we want to do, what it costs, and why it matters.

1. The question

Every solid object has a natural pitch, like a bell. Tap a bell and it rings at one steady note. Physics calls this the object’s resonant frequency.

Standard physics says the note depends on what the object is made of, its shape, its temperature and what surrounds it. It says the note does not depend on where the object sits.

Some researchers propose that an object also carries information about its own place in space and time. If that were true, the same object might ring at a very slightly different note in a different place. This is an idea, not a fact. Nobody has shown it to be true.

2. The experiment

We will build a quiet, carefully controlled test. Two matching copper spheres sit inside a shielded box that is held at a very steady temperature. One sphere stays put. The other is moved between two marked spots.

A laser reads each sphere’s note without touching it. One precise clock keeps every reading in step. The person studying the data does not know which spot is which until the analysis rules are locked. This keeps hopes and guesses out of the result.

Then we ask one thing: do the two notes differ by more than normal error and drift can explain?

3. What it costs and how it is funded

A change this small can only be seen with sensitive equipment and many checks. We plan to raise the money in three phases. Each phase opens only after the one before it is done. All amounts are planning estimates, not price quotes, and no money has been raised yet.

Phase 1: about $86,000

This phase pays for independent expert review of the experiment plan, a statistician, the shielded and temperature-controlled enclosures, rented or partner-lab instruments, legal and accounting costs, the first blinded test, and publishing the results. This is a planning estimate, not a set of price quotes. We will update it when we have real prices.

Phase 2: $450,000 to $1.2 million

Phase 2 is the next step. It builds on what Phase 1 shows. It pays to open and equip a dedicated lab, to buy or rent more of the sensitive instruments, and to hire the experts to run them. The final scope and amount will be published before it opens.

Phase 3: about $2.1 million in total

Phase 3 is the top end of the budget. The $2.1 million is the running total across all three phases, not a separate raise. It covers the full lab, equipment and team needed to run the complete set of experiments and publish the results. The final scope and amount will be published before it opens.

Fundraising has not opened yet. Giving money does not buy ownership, tokens or any financial return. See the full budget and funding plan.

4. Why the result matters

If we find a real difference, it would mean something about how objects relate to space and time is missing from today’s physics. That would be a major discovery. Other labs would need to repeat it before anyone should trust it.

If we find no difference, that matters too. A clean, well-controlled “no” settles a question people keep arguing about. It shows future researchers where not to look. And it shows how to test an unusual idea fairly, instead of simply believing it or dismissing it.

Either way, we publish the data, the analysis code and a kit that lets anyone repeat our work. Good science has to be able to report both answers.

We have no results yet. Nothing on this site claims otherwise.

The technical version: the hypothesis and the null

Standard physics holds that an object's resonance is set by its material, shape, temperature, and surroundings, not by where it is. The Bashar material proposes that location is part of an object's own vibrational makeup. S.P.H.E.R.E. tests that proposal as a hypothesis, not as an assumption.

The Hypothesis

In the Bashar framework, an object's location is described as an intrinsic vibrational variable rather than an external coordinate. If that were true, moving an object might shift its resonant frequency by a small, repeatable amount. This idea is unverified.

The Prediction Being Tested

The source material points to effects above 230 kHz. We will select a well-identified resonance mode in that band and look for a frequency difference (Δf) between two positions larger than a preregistered smallest effect of interest.

The Null Expectation

Standard physics predicts Δf = 0 once temperature, mounting, and other ordinary effects are controlled. If that is what we find, we will publish it as a complete result. Teleportation or "shifting" claims are outside this project's scope.

The First Experiment (LOC-1)

Small frequency shifts are easy to produce by accident through temperature changes, mounting stress, RF interference, or clock drift. The protocol is designed to rule those out before anything is attributed to location.

  • 1

    Matched Reference Sphere

    Two matched hollow copper spheres share one shielded, temperature-controlled enclosure. One never moves. The test sphere is measured as a ratio against it, which cancels shared drift.

  • 2

    Blinded, Randomized Placement

    The test sphere is moved between two marked positions 0.40 m apart on a randomized schedule held by an independent person. The analyst sees only coded labels. Sham moves, rotations, and a dummy object serve as null tests.

  • 3

    Preregistered Analysis

    The primary outcome compares the test-to-reference frequency ratio at A and B. Its analysis rules, including the smallest effect of interest, α = 0.01, and an equivalence test for a bounded null result, will be frozen on OSF before confirmatory data collection. No preregistration has been issued yet. Any candidate signal requires artifact checks and independent replication.

Metrology Approach

Laser Doppler Vibrometry

Rental / partner lab

Non-contact optical readout with no mass loading on the sphere. Instrument time will be rented or arranged through a partner lab rather than purchased.

Thermal Control Enclosure

Project-built

Millikelvin-level temperature control and logging, because copper's resonance shifts measurably with temperature. The temperature coefficient is measured during calibration.

Shielded Enclosure

Project-built

Reduces ambient radio noise in the measurement band. Shielding performance will be measured and published.

GPS / Rubidium Timebase

Shared reference

Every instrument locks to one frequency reference, so clock drift cannot masquerade as a location effect. Hardware guide New

Funding and Transparency

S.P.H.E.R.E. is funded in stages, starting with conventional, non-investment support: donations, sponsorships, grants, and reward-based crowdfunding. Decentralized-science tools may be used later for transparency and community input, but only after the legal entity, financial controls, and first results are in place.

No Tokens, No Equity

Contributions do not buy equity, tokens, royalties, or any financial return. There is no token sale, and none is planned for the founding phase.

Transparent Treasury

Funds will be held in a dedicated project account with documented controls. Spending summaries are published quarterly, and donor funds are kept separate from founder capital.

Open-Source Data

Raw measurements, environmental logs, and analysis code will be published with a permanent DOI, so anyone can check or repeat the analysis.

A careful starting point

What has been demonstrated?

Nothing yet. The project's value depends on a test that can report “nothing found” as honestly as a candidate signal.

Is this established physics?

The locational-variable idea is an unverified, source-inspired hypothesis. S.P.H.E.R.E. is preparing a controlled measurement, not announcing a discovery.

Does supporting the project buy a token or ownership?

No. The founding phase has no token sale or financial-return promise, and the interest form collects no payments.

Does Earth’s motion change a copper sphere’s resonance? New · Oct 9, 2026

Not by itself, as far as established physics goes. Rotation, orbit and galactic motion describe reference frames; they do not on their own predict a copper-sphere frequency shift. The experimental question is whether a specified differential signal survives temperature, mounting, orientation and timing effects. See the Earth-motion article and the discussion of rotating-resonator tests.

Why propose measuring four objects at the same time? New · Oct 9, 2026

Simultaneous readout lets shared drift cancel in a ratio and gives sham and comparator controls over the same observation window. It also adds cost and calibration burden, and it does not remove temperature gradients or mount differences. See the proposed protocol and the error budget.
