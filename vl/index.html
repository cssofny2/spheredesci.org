SPHEREVL

Explore experimental systems through interactive simulations and visual tools.

SPHEREVL is the visual lab product brand of Project S.P.H.E.R.E. Its first featured application, the S.P.H.E.R.E. Control Room, is an educational metrology laboratory simulator for exploring modeled instrument behavior, scan planning and experimental workflows.

What it is

A visual entry point into the lab

SPHEREVL makes scientific, technical and experimental systems easier to explore. The Control Room presents a modeled laboratory experience; it is not a claim that physical equipment is connected or that a research finding has been established.

Instrument workflows

Explore modeled instrument behavior, scan planning and instrument-focused interaction.

Automation and faults

Recorded updates include scan automation and simulated faults for exploring experimental workflows.

Notebook and journal

Recorded updates include notebook functionality and a resizable journal within a compact workspace.

The larger project

How SPHEREVL relates to SPHERE

Project S.P.H.E.R.E. is the parent research initiative. SPHEREVL is its visual lab product brand, and the Control Room is the first featured educational experience.

The broader project explores a locational-variable hypothesis through experimental research. The visual lab provides a way to communicate and explore experimental workflows, but simulated outputs must remain separate from physical experiments, measured datasets and independently replicated findings.

This page is the public overview. lab.spheredesci.org is the official application address. Explore Project S.P.H.E.R.E.

Public questions

SPHEREVL Q&A

What is SPHEREVL?

The visual lab product brand of Project S.P.H.E.R.E., building interactive simulations and visual tools. The Control Room is its first featured experience.

Is this a real laboratory?

The featured Control Room is an educational simulation. Its modeled outputs are not measurements from physical instruments.

Does it prove the SPHERE hypothesis?

No. Simulated outputs do not establish scientific validation or independent experimental replication.

What can I explore?

Modeled instrument behavior and scan workflows. Recorded updates include automation, simulated faults, notebook functionality, a resizable journal and instrument focus.

Who is it for?

People interested in exploring scientific, technical and experimental systems visually. Treat it as an educational application, not a validated research instrument.

Is it finished?

No. SPHEREVL is in active development; features and behavior may change.

Where do I open the application?

Open lab.spheredesci.org. This overview remains at spheredesci.org/vl.

Can I review the source and development?

Yes. Visit the public application repository for source files, commits and issues. Repository visibility alone does not establish particular license permissions.

Does it connect to physical equipment?

This overview does not claim physical hardware connectivity. The verified public description is an educational simulation.

Does the application use analytics?

A Vercel Web Analytics code integration was committed October 5, 2026. That alone does not confirm whether tracking is active or establish the current collection configuration. See the site's privacy information.

Do I need a token?

The reviewed SPHEREVL overview does not describe a token requirement. This page is not an investment or token offering.

How can I contribute?

Submit reproducible bug reports, usability feedback, documentation corrections and feature suggestions through the GitHub issue tracker. Do not include passwords, private keys or sensitive personal information.

Are account access, exports or session saving guaranteed?

This page does not promise account requirements, data export formats, persistent sessions or compatibility with every device. Check the current application interface and documentation.
