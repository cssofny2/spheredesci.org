Spatial Positioning Harmonic Empirical Resonance Experiment

DeSci Whitepaper: Open Resonance Metrology, Digital-Twin Validation, and Accountable Research Funding

Version: 1.0 — Technical and governance draft
Date: October 10, 2026
Project domain: spheredesci.org
Document classification: Proposed research infrastructure and experimental protocol; not a report of experimental findings.

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

1.1 Problem Statement

Project S.P.H.E.R.E. proposes an open experimental program to determine whether the resonance of a controlled copper artifact exhibits a reproducible location-associated change after measured environmental, mechanical, and instrumental effects have been accounted for.

The project addresses three interdependent problems:

  1. Replicability and research transparency. Scientific reproducibility requires accessible methods, adequate statistical power, documented calibration, and publication of negative results. The broader reproducibility problem is documented in the scientific literature; it does not, by itself, establish the validity of any particular unconventional hypothesis. S.P.H.E.R.E. therefore makes preregistration, open data, and independent replication foundational requirements rather than optional dissemination activities. europepmc

  2. Funding bottlenecks. The proposed experiment requires specialist optical instrumentation, controlled environmental infrastructure, and independent technical review before a scientifically interpretable result can be obtained. The project’s financial plans distinguish a small founding pilot from a substantially more expensive dedicated laboratory. Public funding must therefore follow verified technical milestones rather than promises of anomalous outcomes.

  3. Metrology limitations. A resonance measurement above 230 kHz can be affected by temperature-dependent elastic properties, mounting stress, mode identification errors, excitation amplitude, electromagnetic coupling, and timebase drift. Instrument bandwidth alone does not establish sub-hertz measurement accuracy. The relevant quantity is the uncertainty of the complete measurement procedure, including environmental corrections and repeatability.

The central research question is:

Does moving a controlled copper sphere between specified positions produce a repeatable change in its selected resonance, relative to a stationary reference sphere, that exceeds a preregistered effect threshold and survives independent replication?

The October 2, 2026 founding packet identifies the project as pre-entity, pre-funding, and without collected experimental data. This whitepaper retains that status as its documentary baseline; it does not treat illustrative website figures, proposed instruments, or simulated outputs as evidence of funds raised, equipment installed, or discoveries made.

S.P.H.E.R.E. integrates three proposed infrastructure layers:

The simulator is a planning and interpretation system, not an independent source of physical confirmation. Funding is a means of obtaining interpretable measurements, not a mechanism for deciding whether a hypothesis is true.

1.3 Scientific Position and Hypotheses

The project’s locational-variable hypothesis was inspired by the Bashar material presented by Darryl Anka. That source is not established physical evidence. The founding packet explicitly treats it as inspiration for a testable question and excludes claims of teleportation, medical benefit, or financial return.

The confirmatory framework comprises:

  • Null hypothesis, H_0: after preregistered corrections, the location-associated difference lies within the predefined equivalence bounds.
  • Alternative hypothesis, H_1: a difference exceeding the smallest effect of interest persists across randomized returns, survives control experiments, and is independently replicated.
  • Inconclusive outcome: the experiment establishes neither equivalence nor a sufficiently robust difference.

The designation Speculative applies to an intrinsic locational-frequency variable and to any assertion that 230 kHz represents a fundamental physical threshold. In this program, >230 kHz>230\ \text{kHz}>230 kHz is an experimental selection criterion inherited from the hypothesis, not an independently demonstrated boundary in established physics.

2. PHYSICAL METROLOGY & EXPERIMENTAL LAB ARCHITECTURE

2.1 Measurement Definition and Artifact Design

The primary artifact is a thin-walled hollow copper sphere. A matched stationary reference sphere provides a differential measurement channel; a spare and a dummy artifact support reproducibility and null tests. The laboratory cost analysis specifies four high-purity spheres and proposes 99.999% copper. Procurement must separately document chemical purity, oxygen content, fabrication process, wall thickness, surface treatment, and residual stress; these properties must not be inferred from a purity label alone. pplxfilegitgateway-projectfiles-prod-use1.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws

The primary measurand is the resonance-frequency ratio:

where f_T is the selected test-sphere resonance and f_R is the stationary reference resonance.

A vibrometer’s displacement sensitivity is not a guarantee of resonance-frequency uncertainty. Similarly, a VNA’s displayed numerical precision is not equivalent to calibrated accuracy. The complete system must be evaluated through a measurement model and uncertainty budget.

2.3 Excitation and Measurement Channels

The proposed excitation system comprises:

  • A programmable signal generator or qualified integrated vibrometer source.
  • A non-contact excitation mechanism, such as an appropriately designed EMAT or electromagnetic actuator.
  • Drive monitoring through the oscilloscope.
  • A low-noise acquisition or lock-in channel for repeated frequency estimation.
  • Power, amplitude, and duty-cycle controls to limit heating and nonlinear response.

The founding pilot also identifies air-coupled ultrasonic excitation as an option. That method cannot operate through an evacuated propagation path; a vacuum implementation therefore requires a different excitation arrangement or an explicitly separated atmospheric measurement phase.

The optical channel measures mechanical motion. The VNA channel measures the electromagnetic response of the complete sphere–probe–fixture system. Agreement between their fitted features must be demonstrated rather than presumed.

Before assigning a VNA feature to the sphere:

  1. Measure the empty fixture and probe background.
  2. Repeat the measurement with the dummy artifact.
  3. Vary probe separation and orientation.
  4. Compare optical mode shapes and electromagnetic response where a coupling mechanism is physically supported.
  5. Reject features attributable to cables, probes, drive electronics, or environmental interference.

2.4 VNA Calibration and S11S_{11}S11​ Fitting

The proposed VNA workflow is:

  1. Define the calibration reference plane and selected frequency band.
  2. Perform an appropriate calibrated reflection or multiport measurement using characterized standards.
  3. Record cables, adapters, port settings, source power, IF bandwidth, averaging, and calibration identifiers.
  4. Characterize the probe and fixture response separately from the connector-plane calibration.
  5. Fit complex S11S_{11}S11​, not only its magnitude minimum.
  6. Estimate resonance frequency, quality factor, coupling parameters, background terms, and parameter covariance.
  7. Inspect residuals and compare plausible alternative fit models.
  8. Repeat calibration or verification after cable movement, fixture changes, or significant temperature changes.

These requirements extend the project’s proposed near-field VNA measurement into an auditable measurement procedure. Reporting follows the GUM principle that the measurand, measurement model, and associated uncertainty must be explicitly defined.

Neither Q=f0/ΔfQ=f_0/\Delta fQ=f0​/Δf nor a narrow spectral peak alone establishes sub-hertz accuracy. Frequency uncertainty also depends on signal-to-noise ratio, model adequacy, sweep sampling, drift, and repeatability.

2.5 Rubidium Warmup, Lock, and Holdover

The FS725 manufacturer specifies less than 6 minutes to frequency lock and less than 7 minutes to 1×10−91\times10^{-9}1×10−9 after startup under specified conditions. Those figures define reference startup behavior, not readiness of the entire laboratory.

The operating procedure shall require:

  1. Continuous reference operation where practical.
  2. Logged power-on, lock status, and external-reference status.
  3. Verified reference-input compatibility for every receiving instrument.
  4. A distribution arrangement with documented termination, signal level, and channel integrity.
  5. Separate synchronization of acquisition timestamps and triggers; sharing 10 MHz does not by itself synchronize timestamps.
  6. System settling demonstrated by stationary reference measurements before confirmatory acquisition.

Where external 1 PPS disciplining is used, loss of that input shall create a holdover flag. Rubidium operation during holdover must be characterized against an independent reference over the intended run duration. Data shall not be silently represented as externally disciplined after loss of the disciplining signal.

3. S.P.H.E.R.E. SIMULATOR & METROLOGY SOFTWARE

3.1 Role of the Digital Twin

The S.P.H.E.R.E. Simulator is proposed as a reproducible digital twin of the artifact, apparatus, and measurement procedure. Its principal functions are:

  1. Predict conventional resonance modes and their dependence on geometry, material properties, temperature, and boundary conditions.
  2. Estimate measurement sensitivity to mounting, probe position, excitation, and environmental gradients.
  3. Evaluate whether the planned acquisition can distinguish the smallest effect of interest.
  4. Generate controlled synthetic datasets for blinded-analysis testing.
  5. Compare measured behavior with model predictions after calibration.

The reviewed project files establish an interactive browser-based environmental-noise demonstration. They do not establish an experimentally validated digital twin, operational live telemetry, or completed Spatial Field Explorer. Accordingly, the architecture below is a proposed implementation specification rather than a claim of deployed capability. polytec

The software shall distinguish three quantities:

  • Predicted physical response.
  • Measured instrument response.
  • Residual between measurement and the calibrated model.

A residual is not automatically evidence of new physics. It may indicate measurement error, unmodeled environmental effects, or an inadequate model.

3.3 Interactive Room Maps and Spatial Field Explorer

Interactive room maps shall represent the enclosure, fixtures, reference artifact, sensor positions, optical paths, and nominal A/B coordinates in a documented coordinate frame.

The Spatial Field Explorer shall provide:

  • Three-dimensional scalar heatmaps for temperature, vibration magnitude, fitted frequency, or model residuals.
  • Vector displays for quantities actually measured or physically modeled as vectors.
  • Diverging color scales centered on a defined reference value.
  • Visible sensor locations and sampling density.
  • Interpolation method, parameters, and uncertainty.
  • Masks for unsupported extrapolation.
  • Cross-validation diagnostics and sensitivity to interpolation choices.
  • A distinction between measured points and interpolated volumes.

A diverging color scale is a visualization choice; it is not the mathematical divergence of a vector field. Calculation of ∇⋅F\nabla\cdot\mathbf F∇⋅F shall be available only where the field definition, sampling geometry, and differentiation method support it.

3.6 Software Validation and Prototype Remediation

The reviewed environmental-noise prototype modifies plotted amplitudes using heuristic temperature and RF terms, yet includes a message asserting that sufficiently low slider settings eliminate artifacts and make any observed difference genuine. That assertion is not justified by the underlying code and must be removed from research-facing software. polytec

Release qualification shall require:

  1. Unit-consistent equations and documented parameter sources.
  2. Known-input recovery tests for frequency fitting.
  3. Numerical convergence checks.
  4. Null and injected-effect simulations.
  5. Empirical comparison with calibrated artifact measurements.
  6. Removal of unsupported “publication-grade” or “genuine anomaly” classifications.
  7. Explicit labels for illustrative, validated, and speculative models.

4. DeSci GOVERNANCE & CROWDFUNDING MODEL

4.1 Governance Sequence

The project adopts staged decentralization rather than immediate token-based control.

The founding packet specifies founder-led legal and financial accountability, nonbinding advisory participation, no founding-phase tokenization, and no promised equity, royalties, or financial returns. Earlier DAO-oriented materials describe broader token and treasury concepts; those concepts are future options, not established project commitments.

Stage Governance arrangement Funding arrangement
Founding Accountable legal operator with published charter Founder contributions, documented grants, and donation or reward-based funding
Advisory participation Independent scientific reviewers and public proposals Restricted milestone funding

The founding governance review triggers are completion of independent replication, more than 25 active contributors, treasury exceeding $50,000, or passage of 12 months. Transfer of binding authority requires legal review and revised governing documents.

4.2 Decision Rights and Scientific Independence

The governance system shall separate:

  • Legal authority: contracts, custody, compliance, and financial obligations.
  • Scientific authority: calibration, protocol suitability, analysis integrity, and interpretation.
  • Safety authority: immediate suspension of unsafe operations.
  • Community participation: proposals, prioritization, documentation, and advisory review.

Community voting shall not:

  • Alter confirmatory analysis after data collection begins.
  • Suppress null or inconclusive results.
  • Override an unresolved safety issue.
  • Determine whether data establish a physical effect.
  • Give donors control over publication.

These restrictions preserve the founding packet’s commitments to research integrity and disclosure.

A future multisignature treasury may use established infrastructure such as Safe. Signer selection, thresholds, recovery procedures, spending caps, and any contract modules must be approved and tested before custody begins. The general DeSci plan identifies such tools but does not establish that they are deployed for S.P.H.E.R.E. keysight

4.3 Crowdfunding and Milestone Release

Crowdfunding shall finance specified work packages, not an anticipated discovery.

Each funding tranche shall identify:

  1. A bounded scope and acceptance criterion.
  2. Responsible operators and independent reviewers.
  3. Written equipment quotations or service agreements.
  4. Campaign fees and contingency assumptions.
  5. Publication obligations.
  6. The disposition of unused funds.
  7. The procedure for technical failure or schedule revision.

Acceptable milestones include a reviewed protocol, qualified artifact, measured noise floor, completed blinded dataset, or released replication package. A positive anomaly is not an acceptable condition for payment.

The founding policy requires written quotes and public changelog notes for equipment purchases above $1,000, quarterly spending summaries, and separation of founder capital from donor funds.

4.4 Immutable Experiment Logging and Data Hosting

The proposed provenance system shall use:

  1. Instrument-native raw records captured locally.
  2. Append-only storage with retention controls.
  3. SHA-256 hashes for individual records.
  4. Signed manifests linking raw records, configurations, calibration identifiers, and acquisition sessions.
  5. Mirrored public archives.
  6. Optional blockchain anchoring of manifest hashes and version identifiers.

A blockchain commitment can make later changes detectable relative to a published hash. It cannot prove that an instrument was calibrated, that a measurement occurred as described, or that the interpretation is correct.

Large raw datasets shall remain off-chain. IPFS or comparable content-addressed distribution may supplement archival storage, but persistence requires an explicit retention and replication arrangement. The founding packet’s GitHub, OSF, and Zenodo workflow remains the baseline publication pathway.

Corrections shall create new versions with explanations; they shall not overwrite the original experimental record. Personal and security-sensitive information shall be removed before public release.

4.5 Open Peer-Review Protocol

The review workflow shall comprise:

  1. Pre-experiment review: at least two independent reviewers and a statistician examine the protocol and qualification evidence.
  2. Preregistration: freeze hypotheses, mode selection, exclusion rules, primary analysis, significance level, equivalence bounds, and replication requirements.
  3. Blinded analysis: reviewers or analysts receive coded position labels.
  4. Reproduction: independent execution of published analysis against raw records.
  5. Unblinding: release the position key only after the analysis is frozen.
  6. Public reporting: disclose findings, uncertainty, deviations, funding, and conflicts of interest.
  7. Independent replication: require external repetition before a positive public claim.

The founding packet commits to publication within 60 days of unblinding regardless of outcome.

Default proposed licensing is permissive: MIT or Apache-2.0 for software, CC BY 4.0 for protocols and reports, and CC0 or CC BY 4.0 for datasets. Contributor roles shall be recorded; funding alone shall not confer authorship.

5.3 LOC-1 Confirmatory Protocol

The initial experiment shall use the founding packet’s same-enclosure two-position design:

  • A/B separation: 0.40 m center to center.
  • Stationary reference sphere at a third fixed position.
  • At least 40 placements per position, giving 80 principal placements.
  • Ten blocks of eight placements.
  • Randomized, balanced A/B scheduling.
  • Default settling time of 30 minutes, extended if qualification requires it.
  • Environmental logging at at least 1 Hz.
  • Sham moves, rotations, dummy tests, label checks, and reference-only drift segments.

The confirmatory analysis shall include:

  1. A two-sided primary test at α=0.01\alpha=0.01α=0.01.
  2. Preregistered environmental and settling covariates.
  3. Equivalence testing against ± the smallest effect of interest.
  4. Explicit treatment of block structure and serial dependence.
  5. Separate reporting of exploratory and secondary endpoints.
  6. Independent replication before attributing a surviving signal to a reproducible location-associated effect.

Repeated samples within a placement are not substitutes for independent placements or experimental blocks.

A reproducible residual would justify further investigation. It would not uniquely identify an intrinsic location variable: conventional explanations, alternative measurement models, and independent apparatus must still be examined.

A null result would establish a bound for the tested artifact, mode, separation, environment, and sensitivity. It would not refute every possible formulation of an underspecified theory.
